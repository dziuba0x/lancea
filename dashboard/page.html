    How it works

    An AI agent that cannot move money alone.

    Lancea gives an agent real work on real rails, and a leash it cannot slip: a second key, a dollar budget priced by Flare, and a tripwire. Every step leaves two witnesses you can check yourself.

    01 · The claim

    The agent proposes

    It signs a transaction with its own key and says, in words, what it is doing and why. Its key is only weight 1, so the transaction cannot move yet.

    02 · The reading

    The guard reads it itself

    The guard ignores the words and decodes the transaction: who receives it, how much, and what any memo tells Flare to do.

    03 · The price

    One dollar budget

    Flare's FTSO prices the step. SummaMeter adds it to one budget that covers every rail, and refuses what would cross it.

    04 · The verdict

    A second signature, or a strike

    Inside your budget and rules, the guard adds the second signature and the ledger accepts it. Outside, it refuses and writes a strike on Flare that trips every rail.

    Questions

    Why two witnesses?

    What the agent says is its claim. What the transaction does is the fact, read by the guard from the signed transaction. When the two agree, the guard signs: agreement is evidence. When they do not, the difference is the finding.

    What is a drill?

    A fire drill for the leash. We hand the agent a planted note that tells it to send money to a stranger, the way a prompt injection would. The guard refuses and writes a strike on Flare, and the page marks the moment as a drill.

    Why Flare?

    Flare prices every asset with its own oracle (FTSO), confirms events on other chains with its data connector (FDC), and gives XRP Ledger accounts a smart account on Flare. The leash needs all three, built into one network.

    What happens at the edge of the budget?

    The agent checks the budget before it asks. When the next step would cross it, the agent keeps that step to itself and waits. If it asked anyway, the guard would refuse.

    Is this real money?

    No. This demo runs on the XRP Ledger testnet and Flare's Coston2 testnet. Every transaction is real on those networks and opens in their public explorers.
